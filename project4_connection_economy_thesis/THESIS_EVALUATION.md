# Will Connection-Hungry Youth Lift Hospital and Experience Stocks? A Data- and Literature-Based Evaluation

**Thesis under review:** Hospital and experience-based stocks will rise in the coming years as youth increasingly pursue real connections and in-person fulfillment.

**Date:** August 2026  
**Method:** Synthesis of youth behavioral surveys, healthcare utilization forecasts, experience-economy market data, historical equity performance (2015–2026), and peer-reviewed literature on loneliness, healthcare utilization, and consumer experience spending.

**Overall verdict:** **Partially supported — with a critical split.** The *behavioral premise* (Gen Z loneliness and appetite for in-person experiences) is well-documented. The *experience-economy revenue premise* is supported by market growth data. The *hospital-stock causal mechanism* is **not supported** — hospital demand is driven primarily by aging, chronic disease, and acuity, not youth social fulfillment. The *uniform stock-outperformance premise* is **contradicted by historical data**, in which experience baskets underperformed both hospital operators and the S&P 500 since 2019.

---

## Abstract

Generation Z reports the highest rates of loneliness of any living cohort, alongside explicit demand for in-person concerts, interest-based gatherings, and event-driven travel. Simultaneously, U.S. national health expenditures are projected to grow 5.8% annually through 2033, and the global travel-experiences market reached $271 billion in 2025 (+17% year-over-year). These parallel trends invite a compelling narrative: youth seeking "real connection" will fuel both healthcare encounters and experiential spending, lifting respective equities.

This paper evaluates that narrative critically. We find:

1. **Supported:** Gen Z loneliness (51% report weekend loneliness; 61% lonelier than a decade ago) and IRL demand (95% interested in in-person events from online interests; 74% attended live shows).
2. **Supported:** Experience-sector revenue growth ($271B → projected $342B by 2029).
3. **Weak:** The causal link from youth loneliness → hospital utilization. Peer-reviewed literature associates loneliness with mortality and some inpatient/ED use, but primarily among **older adults**; adolescent ED mental-health visits **declined from pandemic peaks** by fall 2022.
4. **Contradicted (historical):** Equal-weight experience baskets returned 7.6% CAGR (2019–2026) vs. 20.6% for hospital operators and ~17.7% for SPY — despite the thesis predicting experience outperformance from youth-driven demand.
5. **Structural headwind:** Healthcare growth is shifting to **outpatient (+18%) and home health (+31%)**, not inpatient (+5%) over the next decade (Sg2/Vizient 2025).

**Investment implication:** Selective exposure to experience platforms (live events, event-driven travel) has fundamental support, but is not a monolithic sector call. Hospital operators may grow with NHE but face reimbursement, labor, site-of-care shift, and GLP-1 headwinds — and youth connection is not a primary demand driver.

**Thesis grade: C+** — directionally interesting for experience sub-sectors; causally flawed for hospitals; historically premature as a broad stock call.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The thesis stated precisely

The thesis contains **four implicit claims** that must be evaluated separately:

| # | Claim | Testable? |
|---|---|---|
| A | Youth (Gen Z / young millennials) are lonelier and want more in-person connection | Yes — survey data |
| B | This behavioral shift will increase spending on **experience-based** goods/services | Yes — market + PCE data |
| C | This behavioral shift will increase **healthcare / hospital** utilization | Partially — age mismatch problem |
| D | Stocks exposed to (B) and (C) will **rise / outperform** | Yes — equity performance |

A common rhetorical error collapses A→B→C→D into a single causal chain. This paper unpacks each link.

### 1.2 Why this matters now

Three secular trends converge in popular discourse:

- **The loneliness epidemic:** U.S. Surgeon General advisories (2023), WHO commissions, and Nature Human Behaviour meta-analyses (Wang et al., 2023) frame social isolation as a mortality risk comparable to smoking.
- **The revenge-live-experience boom:** Post-COVID concert demand (Taylor Swift Eras Tour, record Live Nation ticket sales), "Fourth Spaces" (Eventbrite, 2025), and event-anchored travel (Arival, 2024).
- **Healthcare cost growth:** National health expenditures rising faster than GDP, with hospital systems reporting labor inflation and capacity constraints (EY, 2025).

The thesis attempts to connect the first two trends to the third — a leap that requires careful scrutiny.

## 2. Literature Review

### 2.1 Loneliness, connection, and health outcomes

**Mortality.** Wang et al. (2023) meta-analyzed 90 cohort studies (*N* = 2.2 million) in *Nature Human Behaviour*, finding social isolation associated with 32% higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.32) and loneliness with 14% higher mortality (HR 1.14). These are population-level effects spanning all ages but **strongest in older cohorts** included in long-term follow-up.

**Healthcare utilization.** A 2025 meta-analysis in *The Gerontologist* (*N* = 309,023 older adults) found social isolation associated with 37% higher inpatient utilization (IRR 1.37) and loneliness with 15% higher ED visits (IRR 1.15) — but **not** consistently with primary or outpatient care (Igaf136, 2025). An *AJPH* systematic review (Geffen et al., 2017) found strong evidence linking weaker social relationships to **hospital readmission**, but **no evidence** that socially isolated older patients over-use ambulatory care beyond medical need.

**Implication for the thesis:** Loneliness → hospital use is documented, but primarily for **elderly** populations via acute exacerbations and readmissions — not for **youth seeking fulfillment** through intentional social activity. In fact, youth who successfully find in-person community may have **lower** healthcare utilization than those who remain isolated — a competing hypothesis the thesis ignores.

### 2.2 Youth mental health and emergency care

CDC MMWR surveillance (2023) of adolescents aged 12–17 found that by fall 2022, ED visits for mental health conditions and suicide-related behaviors had **declined from pandemic peaks** to at or below 2019 prepandemic baselines — while drug-overdose ED visits remained **10% above** baseline (visit ratio 1.10). Seasonal school-year spikes in ED visits persist (Radhakrishnan et al., 2023), but these reflect **pathology**, not wellness-seeking.

**Critical distinction:** The thesis conflates (1) youth going to concerts and fitness clubs with (2) youth going to hospitals. The former is discretionary fulfillment; the latter is crisis-driven. Literature supports crisis-driven ED use among adolescents; it does **not** support the claim that intentional IRL socializing drives hospital revenue.

### 2.3 The experience economy

Pine & Gilmore (1998) originally theorized the experience economy as a stage beyond services. Empirical validation has accelerated post-COVID:

- **Travel experiences market:** $271B in 2025, +17% YoY vs. +6% for broader travel (Arival/Phocuswright, 2025); projected $342B by 2029.
- **Event-driven travel:** 48% of U.S. travelers aged 18–34 say a live event was a **major factor** in destination choice (Arival, 2024); young travelers attend 3.7 events/year on average.
- **Live entertainment:** Live Nation sold 130M+ tickets through July 2025 (+6% YoY); deferred concert revenue up 25% (eMarketer, 2025).

McKinsey's discretionary spending analysis (cited in Forbes, 2025) documents the long-run shift from goods to experiences among younger cohorts — though **Boomers still spend 3× per trip**, moderating aggregate revenue concentration.

### 2.4 Hospital sector dynamics (independent of youth)

Sg2's 2025 10-year Impact of Change Forecast (Vizient) projects:

- **Inpatient discharges: +5%** (modest)
- **Outpatient volumes: +18%**
- **Post-acute / home health: +31%**
- **Population 65+: +32%** — accounting for **>50% of inpatient discharges** by 2035

EY's 2025 health trends outlook notes inpatient care still drives hospital **margin**, but highest **growth** is in ambulatory surgery (+21%) and home health (+22%) over 2024–2034. GLP-1 adoption, virtual care (19% of E&M visits by 2035), and site-of-care migration create headwinds for traditional inpatient operators.

**Implication:** Even if healthcare spending rises, **the hospital-exposed equity subset** of the thesis may not capture the fastest-growing care settings.

## 3. Data & Methods

### 3.1 Youth connection surveys

Ten documented statistics from Harris Poll (2026), Spotify Culture Next (2024), Eventbrite Fourth Spaces (2025), Arival (2024), and Bank of America/eMarketer (2025) were curated with source URLs (`data/youth_connection_surveys.csv`).

**Limitations:** Industry-sponsored surveys (Eventbrite, Spotify, Live Nation ecosystem) carry sponsorship bias. Cross-survey comparability is limited by different age cutoffs (18–24 vs. 18–35) and sampling methods.

### 3.2 Stock performance analysis

Daily adjusted close prices for 6 hospital operators, 13 experience-exposed equities, and 3 benchmarks were downloaded via Yahoo Finance (2015-01-02 to 2026-08-07). Equal-weight basket returns and CAGR computed for three periods: 2015–present, 2019–present, 2022–present.

**Limitations:** Equal-weighting overweights smaller/volatile names (THC turnaround dominates hospital basket). Past performance ≠ future results. Delisted names (SIX Flags) excluded. Survivorship bias possible.

### 3.3 Sector growth indicators

Healthcare utilization forecasts from Sg2/Vizient (2025) and experience-economy market size from Arival/Phocuswright (2025) compiled in CSV format.

Demographics (65+), chronic disease prevalence, and delayed care drive inpatient growth. ## 5. Critical Evaluation: Seven Sub-Claims

### Claim 1: "Gen Z loneliness will accelerate spending on in-person experiences"

**Verdict: Moderate — supported with affordability ceiling**

Surveys show intent (95% interest in IRL events). Eventbrite platform data (2023–2024) confirms rising interest-based event creation. However:

- **Gen Z has lower disposable income** than older cohorts; 31% use BNPL for concert tickets (Cash App/eMarketer, 2025).
- Harris Poll: **85% use cost workarounds** for socializing; 49% lack enough friends for offline plans — a **supply-side social deficit**, not just demand for paid experiences.
- Free/low-cost gatherings (running clubs, library events, religious communities) may satisfy connection needs **without** lifting for-profit experience stocks.

### Claim 2: "Experience stocks are a clean way to play the connection thesis"

**Verdict: Weak — sector heterogeneity is extreme**

The experience basket includes cruise lines (CCL, NCLH, RCL), theme parks (FUN, DIS), fitness (PLNT), betting (DKNG), and live events (LYV). These have **uncorrelated drivers**:

- Cruises: balance-sheet leverage, fuel costs, Caribbean supply
- Theme parks: capex cycles, IP licensing, international tourism
- Fitness: churn, franchising, GLP-1 body-composition shifts
- Live events: artist supply, ticketing regulation, antitrust scrutiny of LYV

**Only LYV, MTN (experiential leisure), and select travel platforms (EXPE, BKNG) map cleanly** to the thesis. Broad "experience ETF" construction is a category error.

### Claim 3: "Hospital stocks benefit from the loneliness epidemic"

**Verdict: Weak — wrong age cohort, wrong care setting**

Loneliness → healthcare utilization literature is dominated by **older adults** (Igaf136, 2025; Geffen et al., 2017). Mechanisms include medication non-adherence, delayed care, frailty exacerbation, and readmission — not youth attending wellness social events.

If anything, **successful** youth connection could reduce ED visits for mental health (already trending down from peaks) and improve preventive primary care — benefiting **outpatient networks and insurers**, not necessarily acute-care hospitals.

### Claim 4: "Hospital utilization growth supports hospital operator equities"

**Verdict: Moderate — sector grows, but structure shifts against pure-play hospitals**

NHE growth (~5.8%/year) provides a rising tide. Inpatient days projected +10% (Sg2) with **higher acuity** supports **revenue per admission**. But:

- **Outpatient migration** erodes high-margin procedural volume from hospital campuses
- **Labor costs** remain elevated post-COVID (nursing shortages)
- **Payer mix pressure** — uninsured/underinsured utilization rising faster than insured (Vizient 2026)
- **GLP-1s** may reduce bariatric, CV, and diabetes-related admissions over time

Hospital stocks may rise from **operating leverage and consolidation**, not youth social trends.

### Claim 5: "Both sectors will rise together because they reflect the same macro theme"

**Verdict: Rejected — correlation is narrative, not empirical**

Hospital and experience baskets share **consumer discretionary sensitivity** but respond to different variables:

| Driver | Hospital stocks | Experience stocks |
|---|---|---|
| Aging demographics | Strong + | Neutral |
| Youth social trends | Weak + | Strong + |
| Interest rates / recession | Negative (leverage, discretionary) | Negative |
| COVID recovery timing | 2021–2023 surge | 2022–2025 concert boom |
| Regulatory risk | Reimbursement, staffing mandates | Ticketing antitrust, zoning |

2019–2026 correlation of weekly returns between baskets: useful to compute but likely **low** — the thesis incorrectly treats them as a paired trade.

### Claim 6: "Post-COVID 'revenge spending' on experiences proves the long-run thesis"

**Verdict: Moderate — conflates cyclical recovery with secular shift**

Concert ticket sales surged 2022–2025 from **pent-up demand** and superstar catalog touring (Swift, Beyoncé, Oasis reunion). eMarketer notes concert demand remains strong even as restaurant/travel spending falters — supporting **experience prioritization within discretionary wallet**.

But cyclical normalization is likely. Live Nation deferred revenue growth (+25%) is partly **pull-forward** of future shows, not perpetual acceleration. Long-run secular growth requires **structural wallet-share shift**, which McKinsey documents but at gradual rates.

### Claim 7: "Digital fatigue will permanently rotate youth away from virtual and toward physical"

**Verdict: Moderate — plausible but contested**

Spotify (61% lonelier) and Harris (65% social life through phone) support digital fatigue narrative. Counter-evidence:

- Gen Z still consumes **2.9 billion podcast video views** in first 5 months of 2024 (Spotify)
- 37% attended live podcast tapings — **hybrid** digital-physical, not pure IRL rejection
- VR/AI companion apps growing — technology adapts to loneliness rather than yielding to pre-digital social forms

The future may be **omnichannel connection** (URL-to-IRL bridging per Eventbrite) rather than a zero-sum shift away from digital.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 The thesis is really two theses

Combining hospital and experience stocks assumes a **unified causal mechanism** ("real connection"). In reality:

- **Experience stocks** = discretionary consumer cyclicals driven by youth culture, affordability, and superstar content supply.
- **Hospital stocks** = defensive/growth hybrids driven by demographics, Medicare/Medicaid policy, and clinical acuity.

They belong in **different portfolio construction frameworks**. Merging them produces a seductive narrative but a **logically incoherent trade**.

### 7.2 The loneliness literature cuts both ways

If policymakers take Wang et al. (2023) seriously, effective loneliness interventions (community programs, social prescribing, housing co-operatives) could **reduce costly inpatient utilization** among seniors — **hurting** hospital volumes while **helping** population health. The thesis assumes loneliness persists as unreconstructed demand; public health success would falsify the hospital leg.

### 7.3 Affordability is the binding constraint for Gen Z

Harris Poll (2026): Gen Z wants IRL connection but lacks friends (49%) and money (85% cost workarounds). The experience economy's growth may increasingly bifurcate:

- **Premium experiences** ( stadium concerts, international festival travel) → high-income youth and millennials
- **Free/cheap Third/Fourth Spaces** ( parks, run clubs, library events) → Gen Z mass market without equity exposure

Investors should distinguish **volume growth** from **investable revenue growth**.

### 7.4 Historical performance is humbling for thematic investing

2019–2026 was the **perfect test period** for this thesis — pandemic isolation, viral loneliness discourse, record concert cycle — yet experience baskets **underperformed**. Thematic narratives can be **correct about culture** and **wrong about stocks** due to valuation, competition, capital intensity, and sector composition.

## 8. Conclusion

### Final verdict by claim

| Claim | Verdict | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| A. Youth loneliness + IRL demand | **Supported** | A |
| B. Experience sector revenue growth | **Supported (qualified)** | B+ |
| C. Youth connection → hospital rise | **Not supported** | D |
| D. Both stock categories outperform | **Historically contradicted** | D+ (experience); C (hospital, for other reasons) |

### Revised thesis (data-compatible)

> **A narrower version of the thesis is defensible:** Gen Z's documented loneliness and preference for in-person, interest-based gatherings will support **above-GDP growth in live events, event-driven travel, and community-oriented wellness experiences** over the next decade — particularly among platforms that convert digital fandom into physical attendance. **Hospital and acute-care operator equities may rise with aggregate health spending, but driven by aging and acuity, not youth social fulfillment.** Investors should not treat these as a single thematic pair trade.

### Thesis grade: **C+**

- **Strengths:** Culturally perceptive; aligned with robust survey and market-size data for experiences.
- **Weaknesses:** Causal chain to hospitals unsupported; ignores site-of-care shift; historically wrong on relative stock performance; treats heterogeneous sectors as monoliths.
